Offtake Partners

Capital that follows contracts.

We invest in Vietnam's construction-materials supply chain, and only where the output is already sold.

Why offtake

Vietnam is in the largest build-out in its history: expressways, airports, the North–South railway and a million social-housing units before 2030. Every kilometre and every unit is made of stone, aggregates, brick and concrete, and leaves behind construction waste that someone has to process.

The businesses that supply these materials are mostly family-owned, fragmented and under-capitalised. They have demand on their doorstep and no structured way to finance the plant, the licences and the governance that demand requires.

An offtake agreement changes that. When the buyer is contracted before the plant is built, the investment is anchored to a committed revenue stream rather than to a forecast. That is the only kind of investment we make. We do not invest ahead of demand. We invest behind a signed contract.

How we invest

  1. Offtake first, capital second. Every investment starts with a contracted buyer. No contract, no capital.
  2. Diligence before price. Weeks on site, with the buyers and the books, before any discussion of valuation.
  3. Licences complete; reserves and capacity independently assessed. We do not price what we cannot verify.
  4. Operators, not spectators. Our team has run plants and quarries. We sit beside management, not above it.
  5. Structured for scrutiny. Singapore vehicles, independent audit and international anti-corruption standards, so that partners and lenders can look at everything.

From contract to plant

  1. Contract. Find a buyer with a funded programme: an expressway package, a public developer, a housing scheme. Secure the offtake.
  2. Diligence. Verify the supplier: licences, reserves, equipment, books, people.
  3. Capital. Structure the investment against the contract, alongside a lender where one is needed.
  4. Operate. Deliver on the contract, hold the standards, and build the next one.

Where contracted demand exists

  • Construction-waste recyclingDemand set by disposal rules and by contractors who need recycled aggregate.
  • Stone and aggregates for infrastructureDemand set by expressway, rail and airport packages.
  • New materials for social housingDemand set by the national housing programme.
